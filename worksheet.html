Finding pin 14

Both long edges have 36 pins. They look identical. Pin 1 is at the end nearest the USB socket, and the numbers zig-zag: odds on one row, evens on the other.

Count in pairs along the bottom row: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14. Seven pairs. Count out loud. Count twice.

Pin numbers here are inherited from the original PocketBeagle. Check them against the PocketBeagle 2 diagram before you wire anything.

Ten steps

Tick each one as you finish it. If a step will not work, go to the troubleshooting list before you undo anything.

1
Count the parts Six kinds of thing. Eight wires, not seven.
2
Card in first Short edge, opposite the USB socket. Contacts face the board. It sits almost flush.
3
Plug in and wait Four lights come on. One flickers unevenly, which is good. Then nothing visible for about 30 seconds. That is normal.
4
Unplug again Never move a wire while the board has power.
5
Find pin 14 and pin 15 Use the diagram on page 1. Count out loud, in pairs, twice.
6
Wire power and ground 14 → 3V3. 15 → GND. On the sensor and on the screen.
7
Wire data and clock 26 → SDA. 28 → SCL. Same two pins serve both devices.
8
Power up and scan the bus Run sudo i2cdetect -y <bus>. Look for 76 and 3c.
9
Check the chip Run sws-check. It reads the sensor's ID register and tells you what you actually have.
10
Read your own weather Press the button to cycle the three pages on the screen.
