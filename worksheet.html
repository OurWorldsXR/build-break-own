Finding pin 14

Both long edges have 36 sockets. They look identical. Hold the board with the sockets up: the strip with the tiny printed 1 and 2 at the USB-C end is P1. Pin 1 is at that end, and the numbers zig-zag: odds along the outer row, evens along the inner row. Pin 14 is the 7th socket along the inner row; 15 is the 8th along the outer row; 26 and 28 are the 13th and 14th along the inner row.

Count in pairs along the bottom row: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14. Seven pairs. Count out loud. Count twice.

Pin numbers here are inherited from the original PocketBeagle. Check them against the PocketBeagle 2 diagram before you wire anything.

Ten steps

Tick each one as you finish it. If a step will not work, go to the troubleshooting list before you undo anything.

1
Count the parts and pick your kit Breadboard in the bag: Kit A, twelve wires. No breadboard: Kit B, four wires.
2
Lay it out Board sockets up, USB-C on the left, so P1 is the top strip and pin 1 is at the left end. Modules above it, pins toward you. Write down your screen's pin order.
3
Find pins 14, 15, 26, 28 Count columns from the pin 1 end: 14 is column 7 inner, 15 is column 8 outer, 26 is column 13 inner, 28 is column 14 inner. Count out loud, twice. P1.1 is 5 V: nothing goes in it.
4
Kit A: seat the modules, then four wires from the board Sensor pins in row j, columns 1 to 4; screen in row j, columns 9 to 12. Then 14 → column 14, 15 → 15, 26 → 16, 28 → 17, all in row h. Those four columns are now the shared wires.
5
Kit A: sensor, then screen, into the shared columns Sensor in row f: VIN → 14, GND → 15, SDA → 16, SCL → 17. Screen in row g, in YOUR screen's pin order. Red must end at VIN or VCC, black at GND.
6
Kit B: four wires straight to the sensor Pin end in the board, socket end on the module. 14 → VIN, 15 → GND, 26 → SDA, 28 → SCL. The screen gets the same four wires later, on its own.
7
Card in, then plug in, then wait Card slot is on the socket side, next to the USB-C end, label facing out. Lights are on the underside. About 40 seconds before anything happens. Kit A: the screen comes on by itself.
8
Reach it from the laptop ssh student@192.168.7.2 (Mac: 192.168.6.2), password buildit.
9
Prove it Run sws-check. Five questions in plain language. Question 2 should list 0x76 (the sensor) and, Kit A, 0x3C (the screen). Question 3 should say "real BME280". The long way: sudo i2cdetect -y -r 2.
10
Read your own weather Run sws-live. Breathe on the sensor and watch humidity climb. Kit A: the screen changes page every five seconds on its own.
